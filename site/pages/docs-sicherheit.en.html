Sign-in & security

Streamo stores no credentials for third-party services and sends no emails. Your data stays on your server.

Three ways to sign in

WayWith
Username + passwordalways works, everywhere
Email + passwordthe email is just a second sign-in name
PasskeyWindows Hello, Face ID, fingerprint or security key

With a passkey the private key never leaves your device – Streamo only stores the public part. Even someone who steals the whole database can't sign in with it. Set it up under Settings → Passkeys; several devices are possible, and the password can be removed afterwards.

Two-factor sign-in

A six-digit code from an authenticator app (Aegis, 2FAS, Google Authenticator …) in addition to the password, per RFC 6238 and checked against its official test vectors. You also get ten backup codes, shown exactly once – keep them safe, because without email there is no other way back.

Accounts and invitations

The first account from the setup wizard becomes the administrator. Others join via invitation link – single-use or permanent for the whole family – and need no TMDB access of their own. Open registration is off on purpose: otherwise anyone who finds the address could create an account.

Passkeys need HTTPS and a host name

The browser insists: no passkey over http:// (except localhost) and none via a bare IP address. The easiest way is a reverse proxy with a Let's Encrypt certificate (Nginx Proxy Manager, Traefik, Caddy) in front of http://<container-ip>:3000, then in the container:

streamo domain streamo.your-domain.com

The command sets TRUST_PROXY, WEBAUTHN_RP_ID and WEBAUTHN_ORIGIN and restarts. Without a proxy, ENABLE_HTTPS=true with a self-signed certificate works too.

Passkeys are bound to one domain – that's what makes them phishing-proof. If you move to another domain you have to create them again; the password keeps working.

Under the hood

  • Passwords with scrypt, server-side sessions – "sign out" takes effect immediately.
  • Strict Content Security Policy, HSTS, rate-limited sign-in attempts.
  • The TMDB key never leaves the server, not even masked.
  • Two npm dependencies in total: express and @simplewebauthn/server.
